# Capital Allocation Intelligence (CAI) — Plain-English Breakdown

**Date:** 2026-07-15
**Status:** Planning

---

## The Big Idea (skip this, start here if new to investing)

MRI helps you find good stocks to buy.

CAI helps you decide what to do with them **after** you buy them.

That sounds simple, but the vast majority of people lose money not because they picked the wrong stock — they lose money because they didn't know what to do after buying. They hold too long. They sell too early. They add to a falling stock hoping it will come back. They don't take profits when they have them. They never move their stop loss up.

CAI acts like a coach sitting next to you. Every day it tells you:

> "Here is what you should do with **each stock you own** today."

It does not make emotional decisions. It follows the same rules every time.

---

## The 5 Questions CAI Answers

### 1. Should I buy this now?

Before you spend any money, CAI checks:

- Is the weekly trend healthy?
- Is volume confirming the move?
- Is the stock breaking out of a range?
- Are we in a good market for buying?

**Plain meaning:** CAI stops you from buying a stock that looks good on paper but is actually entering a bad setup. It's like checking the weather before going on a trip — even if the destination is nice, you don't go during a hurricane.

---

### 2. Does it deserve more money?

You bought ₹20,000 worth of a stock.

Now you're thinking: should I buy ₹20,000 more?

Most people do this emotionally:

- Stock went up? "I'm a genius, add more!" (might be too late)
- Stock went down? "It'll come back, buy the dip!" (averaging down into a losing trade)

CAI follows fixed rules:

1. Is your first ₹20,000 showing a profit right now? (If not, you cannot add.)
2. Is the weekly trend still intact? (If it broke, you cannot add.)
3. Is a better stock available for that ₹20,000 instead?

**Plain meaning:** CAI treats your money like a worker. If the first worker earned their keep, they can bring a friend. If the first worker is losing money, no more workers until they turn things around. You never double down on a mistake.

---

### 3. Should I protect my profits?

You bought at ₹1,000. It's now ₹1,200. You have a ₹200 profit.

Should you:

- Do nothing?
- Move your stop loss up to ₹1,100 (protect some profit)?
- Take half the profits and let the rest run?
- Exit entirely?

CAI looks at:
- Is the weekly trend still strong? → Hold or trail stop up
- Is the stock losing momentum? → Raise stop to protect gains
- Has the trend broken? → Exit

**Plain meaning:** You grew a plant from seed. It's now flowering. CAI tells you when to water it, when to protect it from wind, and when it's time to harvest. It prevents you from either picking the flower too early (small profit) or letting it rot on the vine (profit turns to loss).

---

### 4. Should I sell something to buy something better?

You own 5 stocks. A new stock appears that looks excellent.

You only have ₹20,000 cash.

CAI asks:

- Should you buy the new stock with cash?
- Or should you reduce one of your existing holdings and use that money?

This is called **rotation**. CAI compares each stock you own with the new candidate and tells you which deserves the capital most.

**Plain meaning:** Imagine you have 5 employees. A brilliant new candidate applies for a job. You can either hire them with the budget you have, or replace your weakest employee with them. CAI tells you which employee underperforms and should be let go.

---

### 5. Should I exit completely?

Sometimes the right thing to do is nothing.

Sometimes it's to sell.

CAI tells you when:

- The trend has broken → Exit
- A superior opportunity exists → Rotate into it
- You've held too long and momentum is dying → Reduce or exit
- Risk is too high (concentration, bad market) → Reduce

**Plain meaning:** CAI doesn't sugarcoat. If a position is dead, it tells you to sell. It prevents the most common mistake: holding a losing stock for months because you're "waiting for it to come back."

## Phase 1: Position Health + Action Matrix + Decision Ledger

**Time estimate:** 5-6 days

### Task 1.1 — Position Health Score (0-100)

**What it does:** Every stock you own gets a score. 100 means it's your best holding. 0 means it's your worst.

**What goes into the score:**

- **Trend quality** — Is the weekly chart still making higher highs and higher lows? Think of this as: is the car still moving forward?
- **Profit quality** — How much profit do you have? What's the peak profit you had vs current profit? This prevents giving a high score to a stock that went from ₹100 to ₹200 and is now back at ₹110 (that looked great once but the profit is mostly gone now).
- **Drawdown** — How far is the stock from its recent high? A stock that's ₹10 below its all-time high is healthy. A stock that's ₹100 below its high is in trouble.
- **Time in trade** — How long have you held it? Fresh positions and very old positions are treated differently. A stock you bought yesterday hasn't proven itself yet. A stock you've held for 6 months that hasn't moved anywhere is wasting capital.
- **Sector concentration** — Do you own too many stocks in the same industry? If the whole sector drops, you get hit from all sides. CAI reduces the score for stocks in an overweighted sector.
- **Position concentration** — Is too much of your money tied up in one stock? If one stock is 40% of your portfolio and it falls 20%, you lose 8% of everything. CAI reduces the score for oversized positions.

**Why a new investor should care:** Instead of guessing "is my stock doing well?", you get a single number. 92? Great, it's your best holding. 41? Something is wrong, pay attention.

---

### Task 1.2 — Action Matrix

**What it does:** Based on the score, CAI tells you exactly what to do.

| Score | Action | What it means in plain language |
|-------|--------|----------------------------------|
| 90-100 | ADD | Add more money. This stock has earned it. |
| 75-89 | HOLD | Do nothing. It's fine. Let it keep working. |
| 60-74 | WAIT | Something isn't right. Don't add. Don't exit. Just wait and watch. |
| 40-59 | REDUCE | Sell some of it. Reduce your risk. Either the stock or your plan has changed. |
| <40 | EXIT | Sell all of it. The reasons you bought are no longer true. |

**The hard rules that override the score:**

These are ironclad. No matter the score:

- You cannot ADD if your first purchase is showing a loss
- You cannot ADD if the weekly trend has broken
- You cannot ADD if a superior candidate exists (MRI score 95+)

**Why a new investor should care:** The scores are helpful, but the hard rules protect you from yourself. Your score might say 92, but if the weekly trend just broke, CAI says WAIT, not ADD. The system prevents you from making an emotional mistake even when the numbers suggest otherwise.

---

### Task 1.3 — Decision Ledger

**What it does:** Every single decision CAI makes — BUY, ADD, HOLD, REDUCE, EXIT, ROTATE — is recorded with:
- The date and stock
- The CAI score and action at that time
- The reasons (which rules were satisfied or violated)
- The outcome later (was it a good decision?)

**Why a new investor should care:** This is a learning tool. Months later, you can look back and see:

- "In July, CAI said REDUCE HomeFirst at score 43. I ignored it. I lost ₹8,000."
- "In June, CAI said WAIT on Navin. I waited. It dropped 15% next week. Good call."
- "Which rules produced the best outcomes? Which decisions did I override and regret?"

Most people never review their past decisions. They make the same mistake 50 times. The Decision Ledger breaks that cycle.

## Phase 2: Portfolio Layer

**Time estimate:** 3-4 days

### Task 2.1 — Sector Exposure Scanner

**What it does:** Looks at everything you own and tells you:

- You own 4 pharma stocks → that's 60% of your portfolio
- Pharma sector drops 10% → you lose 6%
- Recommendation: don't add more pharma stocks

**Why a new investor should care:** Beginners often buy stocks they like without realizing they've accidentally put all their money in one industry. If that industry has a bad month, their entire portfolio suffers. This scanner catches that.

---

### Task 2.2 — Rotation Engine

**What it does:** You have 5 stocks. A new stock enters your watchlist with MRI score 98. You have ₹20,000 cash. Should you use the cash or sell something?

CAI compares:

- Each holding's CAI score vs the new candidate's MRI score
- If a holding scores below 50, it suggests reducing it and deploying into the new stock
- If nothing scores below 60, it suggests waiting (no need to force a trade)

**Why a new investor should care:** Most people either (a) ignore new opportunities because they're already invested, or (b) chase everything and own 30 mediocre stocks. CAI helps you upgrade your portfolio one position at a time.

---

### Task 2.3 — Opportunity Cost Score

**What it does:** Every rupee tied up in a stock has an opportunity cost — that rupee could be in a better stock.

CAI scores this explicitly. If you own a stock with CAI 58 and a new candidate has MRI 96, that's a massive opportunity cost. CAI flags it.

**Why a new investor should care:** You might think "my stock is doing fine, I'll keep it." But "fine" isn't good enough when something great is available. CAI makes that visible.

## Phase 3: Risk Management

**Time estimate:** 2-3 days

### Task 3.1 — Trailing Stop Engine

**What it does:** Instead of a fixed stop loss (always sell at -8%), the stop moves up as the stock rises.

Example:
- Buy at ₹1,000
- Stock goes to ₹1,200
- Weekly swing low is ₹1,120
- Stop moves to ₹1,100
- If stock drops below ₹1,100, exit with ₹100 profit instead of ₹0

**Why a new investor should care:** The biggest frustration for beginners is watching a ₹200 profit turn into ₹0 because they didn't move their stop. The trailing stop engine does this automatically and intelligently — not at a random percentage, but at the level that makes sense for the stock's current structure.

---

### Task 3.2 — Drawdown Protection

**What it does:** If a stock drops too far from its recent high, CAI signals REDUCE or EXIT regardless of the score.

**Why a new investor should care:** This prevents the "it'll come back" trap. A stock that falls 25% needs to rise 33% just to break even. Most don't. CAI tells you to cut losses before they become catastrophic.

## Phase 6: Validation

**Time estimate:** 2-3 days

### Task 6.1 — Backtest

**What it does:** Runs CAI's rules against historical data. For every stock you owned in the past, it shows: "If you had followed CAI in January, it would have told you to _____ on February 3rd. Then the stock did _____. Result: +₹X or -₹Y."

**Why a new investor should care:** Trust is built through evidence. This shows you that the system would have made money in the past. It's not a guarantee of the future, but it's better than hoping.

---

### Task 6.2 — Calibration

**What it does:** Adjusts the thresholds (what counts as a "good" score vs "bad") based on how the system performs in real use. If CAI says REDUCE too often and the stock goes up every time, the calibration raises the REDUCE threshold. If it says WAIT and the stock crashes, it lowers the WAIT threshold.

**Why a new investor should care:** The system learns from real outcomes. It gets smarter over time.

---

### Task 6.3 — Dry Run

**What it does:** CAI runs on your live portfolio but doesn't actually suggest actions. It shows you "what would CAI have said" for a week. You watch the recommendations without acting on them. At the end of the week, you decide whether to trust it.

**Why a new investor should care:** This is a test drive. You see the system in action with your actual portfolio before committing to follow its advice.
